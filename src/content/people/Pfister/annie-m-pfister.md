# Annie M. Pfister

**Lifespan:** November 1846 – c. 1906

## Summary
Born November 1846 in **Württemberg**, Germany (1880 census image), to Württemberg-born parents. Called **Christiana** in 1880 and Annie in 1900. She was the second wife of **John Pfister Sr.** of Fish Creek, Door County, and married him about 1872 (28 years married in 1900). She had 9 children, 8 of them living in 1900. She died about mid-1906, before John. Mother of Margaret Pfister (1876–1962), so a 3×-great-grandmother of Don Krumpos through Hazel P. Mielke.

The full evidence is in [[john-pfister-sr]] and [[margaret-pfister]]. This profile exists so the pedigree links to her.

## Relationships
- **Husband:** John Pfister Sr. (1830–1908)
- **Children:** Margaret (1876–1962) is the direct line. For the rest, see john-pfister-sr.md; which children were hers and which were the first wife's is not settled.

Read: all *Advocate* issues 23 Mar–Dec 1906 (IDs 00000535–576) and all *Democrat* issues Feb–Nov 1906 (00000685–725), searching for Pfister/Pflster/Plister/Pfisterer/Fister/Weidner/"Mrs. John P". **No death or funeral notice for her.** All 1903–08 "Pfister" hits and every 1905–07 "asylum"/"insane" item were also checked; the only Fish Creek committal is Mrs. Mary Larson (June 1905, to the Northern Hospital). The second obituary (above) repeats "about one year and a half ago". A death that went unreported locally fits a death away from home, e.g. in a state or county hospital (cf. the 1880 "Insane" tick), so the WHS hospital-records item on the offline list remains the best route.

Next: the WHS state-hospital and county-asylum records (offline list); the Brown County Asylum at Green Bay is a possibility as well as the Northern Hospital.

Asylum inmates are often indexed by initials or omitted, so the WHS hospital registers (offline list) remain the test.
- **2026-10-14 s6 — absent from the 1905 household; Washington Co. lead weakened.** The 1905 WI census (Gibraltar, Door Co.) has John "Phfister" (b. 1830) with Mary (1873), Frank (1884) and "Canfer" [Casper] (1893), and **no wife**. So by 1905 she was dead or living away, perhaps in a hospital (cf. the 1880 "Insane" tick). No Pfister/Pfisterer woman b. 1843–50 is indexed anywhere in WI in 1905. The Washington Co. candidate (VJGT-8Q2, d. 22 May 1906) is probably the **Anna Pfister b. 1853, Germany, living at West Bend in 1905** (FS MMQX-WH7), a local woman, not Annie. That lead is weak now. If Annie died before 1905, her death falls between June 1900 and 1905, which contradicts the obituary's "about a year and a half" before Jan 1908; the hospital explanation (alive but away) fits both.
- **Given name [2026-10-13]:** the 1880 census calls her **Christiana**, b. 1846. That fits the 1900 index's "Annie **C**" (the image was earlier read as "Annie M"). She was probably Anna Christiana (or Christiana Anna). John Jr. (b. 1871) and Anna (b. 1870) are in her 1880 household, and nothing in the census marks them as stepchildren. The "married c. 1872" from 1900 may be off by two or three years, and the first wife may have had no surviving children. The 1880 image would tell (it has a relationship column).
- **1880 census image read [2026-10-13 s3]** (FS MN4X-DJ7; Nasewaupee ED 43 p. 3, dwelling 16): "Christiana", 34, wife, keeping house, b. **Württemberg**, both parents b. Württemberg. All five children (Anna 10, John 9, Mary 7, Margaretha 5, William 3) are son/daughter of the head, b. Wisconsin, with mother b. Württemberg. That favours her as the mother of all five. If she spent her last years in a state hospital (Northern Hospital, Winnebago, or Mendota, Madison), her c. 1906 death would be recorded in that county, not Door.
