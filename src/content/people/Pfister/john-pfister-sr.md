# John Pfister Sr.

**Lifespan:** June 19, 1830 – January 2, 1908

## Summary
Born June 19, 1830 in **Württemberg**, Germany, very probably at **Zuffenhausen** near Stuttgart as Johannes, son of David Pfisterer × Margaretha Schober (baptised 27 Jun 1830; FS index) (the obituary's "Wittenberg"; the 1880 census image says Württemberg). Emigrated to America in 1853. Arrived in Door County, Wisconsin in the late 1850s with brother Phillip Pfister — among the very first settlers in the county. Located on lands in the northwestern part of Nasewaupee township. Phillip died in the Civil War in 1863; John stayed behind to care for both families. Moved to the northern part of Door County (Gibraltar) in 1880. Twice married, both wives preceded him in death. Eight surviving children. Died January 2, 1908 in Gibraltar, Door County, Wisconsin, age about 77.

## Obituary (full text)
PFISTER — In the town of Gibraltar, Door county, Wis., January 2, 1908, JOHN PFISTER, Sr., aged about 77 years.

It was in the latter part of the 50s that John Pfister and his brother Phillip came to this county, and located on lands in the northwestern part of Nasewaupee, being among the very first to penetrate the wilderness, which stretched in an unbroken line in all directions. In 1863 Phillip having been summoned by an imperilled government to assist in subduing an unholy rebellion, his brother John remained behind to look after his own family and that of the absent one. The latter having died in the line of duty in the south, John remained on the place until 1880, when he moved to the northern part of the county, which has been his home ever since. He was born in Wittenberg, Germany, June 19, 1830. He came to America in 1853. He was twice married, both wives having preceded him, the last one about a year and a half ago. The surviving children are eight in number, namely: John, Green Bay; William, L'Anse, Mich.; Mesdames Matt. Felhofer, Sevastopol; Emil Meikle, Clay Banks; Louis Miller, Sturgeon Bay town; Richard Lyman, Gibraltar; Miss Mary and Frank. Owing to the failure of the letter, which was dispatched announcing his death, to reach the relatives in this vicinity, these did not know of the passing away of their father until after the funeral had occurred, but they nevertheless made the trip to the home of the deceased, reaching there two days after the obsequies. Mr. Pfister was an industrious and hard-working man, who had a great deal to contend with owing to a long illness in his family, and he has gone to his final home mourned by grateful children and other near relatives.

## Research Notes
- **2026-10-16 s17 — German origin found: Zuffenhausen, son of David Pfisterer × Margaretha Schober (PROBABLE).** FS index: Johannes Pfisterer, b. **19 Jun 1830** (the obituary's exact date), chr. 27 Jun 1830 at **Zuffenhausen**, Oberamt Ludwigsburg, next to Stuttgart. His parents' other children include **Johann Philipp (b. 6 Feb 1824)** and **David (b. 15 Oct 1827)**. So both of the "brother" puzzles are resolved: John had a brother David (d. Mobile 1865, widow Margaret Kleinknecht of Stuttgart) **and** a brother Philipp (the Philip Pfister who died at Little Sturgeon on 4 Jan 1869). Philipp married **Catharine Christine Weidner** (b. 18 Jun 1836, Waldbach, Oberamt Weinsberg; daughter of Leonhard Christian Weidner × Margaretha Gottliebin Stiegele) on 18 Feb 1862 at Waldbach (index), which matches the Door Co. widow "Cathrine Pfisterer, dau. of Christian Weidner" who married Henry Bourton in 1869. So Philipp emigrated after Feb 1862, not in the 1850s as the obituary has it. John's wife Christiana/Annie is probably her sister **Christiana Christina Carolina Weidner, b. 16 Nov 1845** (see [[annie-m-pfister]]). Parents added as PROBABLE stubs ([[david-pfisterer-1796]], [[margaretha-schober]]); the grandparents (Johannes Pfisterer × Maria Magdalena Sigel; Johann Georg Schober × … Pfizer) stay in prose. ### Immigration and settlement
- Born in **Wittenberg, Germany** per the obituary — but **"Wittenberg" is now suspected to be a garble of Württemberg** [2026-07-15]: Pfister/Pfisterer is a southern-German (Swabian) occupational surname ("baker"), the first-generation family spelling was the even-more-Swabian **Pfisterer**, and sister-in-law Margretha Kleinknecht was born in **Stuttgart**, the Württemberg capital. Unproven until an emigration or church record surfaces — test via the Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg emigration database (leo-bw.de → Auswanderer module → Personensuche; search "Pfister" and "Pfisterer," emigration 1850–1856, destination North America; the form is session-bound and must be searched manually in a browser).
- **Emigrated 1853** — age 23. Arrived in Door County late 1850s. 5+ years between arrival in America and settling in Door County — may have lived elsewhere first.
- Among the **very first settlers** in Door County — located on wilderness land in Nasewaupee before the Civil War. Pioneer family.
- Moved from Nasewaupee (southern Door County) to Gibraltar (northern Door County, near Fish Creek/Ephraim) in 1880.

### Civil War connection — brother identified as David Pfisterer [REVISED 2026-07-15]
The obituary's "Phillip, died 1863 in the line of duty in the south" doesn't match any record. The actual Civil War Pfisterer from Door County:

- **David Pfisterer** (indexed as "Pfesterer" in the NPS Soldiers & Sailors database, film M559 roll 23) — Pvt, **Co. E, 11th Wisconsin Infantry**. Wisconsin Veterans Museum roster: "DRAFTED; DIED AUGUST 2, 1865, MOBILE, ALABAMA, DISEASE," enlisted (drafted) Mar 23, 1865. Confirmed in the printed *Roster of Wisconsin Volunteers* (1886). Buried **Mobile National Cemetery, Mobile, AL, Plot 2, Grave 425** (VA interment records; duplicate entries under Pfisterer/Pfiserer).
- **Widow:** Margretha (Kleinknecht) Pfisterer — b. Oct 17, 1829, **Stuttgart** (Württemberg); married David **1853 in New York** (same year as John's emigration); remarried **John Bink Sr., Apr 10, 1866, Door County** (WHS index MR2175478, Vol 1 p. 19). Per Peninsula Genealogical Society Bink/Pfisterer family page (descendant-contributed — secondary), she walked from Nasewaupee to Sturgeon Bay through the 1871 fire devastation; d. Nov 4, 1912; unmarked grave, Bink plot, St. Joseph's Catholic Cemetery, Sevastopol.
- **David's children (WHS pre-1907 birth index, Door County, reel 50 — consecutive record numbers suggest a batch/delayed registration):** John b. Aug 9, 1854 (rec 2707); David b. Aug 6, 1857 (rec 2708); **Philip b. Oct 29, 1863 (rec 2711)** — plus Mary 1859, Casper 1860, William 1861, Peter 1862, Catherine 1865 in the wider Pfisterer birth index (counties unverified). The 1863-born son Philip is the probable source of the obituary's "Phillip… 1863."
- **How the obituary garbled it (reading, not proof):** written 45 years later — brother's name swapped for nephew's, death year pulled toward the son's 1863 birth, "died in the south" correct (Mobile).
- **Negative evidence (all confirmed):** no Phillip Pfister/Fister/Phister/Pfeister/Pfisterer in any Wisconsin unit (NPS index + WVM database + 1886 printed roster); no Pfister in Holand's Door County Roll of Honor (167 names); no Pfister on the 1883 Door County pensioners list — expected, since the widow's pension ended at her 1866 remarriage and minors' pensions ran out by ~1881.
- **Settled 2026-10-15 s11:** the *Advocate* of 12 Dec 1903 (image read) says John Pfister and the first husband of Mrs. Margaret Binck (= Margaret Kleinknecht, David's widow, m. John Bink 1866) "were brothers". So **David Pfisterer was John's brother**. (Earlier caveat: David-as-brother rested on the PGS descendant page plus record convergence.) The pension file is still worth ordering for the German origin.
- The first-generation surname was consistently **PFISTERER**, shortened to Pfister — which corroborates Margaret's 1902 marriage record spelling her surname "Pfisterer."
