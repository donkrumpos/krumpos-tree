This is the best-supported Celtic thread found in the whole tree so far. It is 11 generations back, which is about 0.05% by pedigree arithmetic.

## 2. Louise LAMONT (Jacques G. Bodoh's mother) — Scottish or French?

Lamont is a Scottish clan name (Cowal, Argyll), but it is also a French habitational name (*l'Amont*), and in Quebec it is a variant of Lamonde/Lamontagne. Clinton County, NY, around 1812 had both French-Canadian refugee families and Scots. **UNRESOLVED.** This is the one Celtic possibility close to the Bodoh line, and it rests entirely on the Hogansburg/Plattsburgh registers already on the offline batch list.

## 3. Cobb "Land's End, Cornwall" — family tradition only (SPECULATIVE)

WikiTree (`Cobb-524`, `Cobb-2744`) has Asa Cobb I born 1701 and Asa II born 1724 at "Land's End, Cornwall". The story is that they ran a whaler out of Newfoundland and were detained off New York in 1766. Both profiles cite only Ancestry member trees. WikiTree's own biography says the story "isn't likely to be accurate", because Asa II had a child at Wallkill by 1748. The Y-DNA does not match Samuel Cobb. This is probably the source of the "immigrant c.1766" in Sherwood's *Descendants of Asa Cobb* (FS DL 131993).

## 5. Other Celtic-adjacent hits in the Cobb pedigree (not Celtic)

The WikiTree Cobb pedigree (162 people, through Mary Finch, Sarah Stephens and Elsa Holloway) is New England Puritan. It includes the Mayflower passengers **George Soule** and **Francis Eaton**. It also includes **Hoskins** and **Winthrop** of **Aghadown, Co. Cork** (William Hoskins b. 1611 Aghadown; Anne Winthrop b. 1596 there). That is the English Winthrop plantation in Munster, so they were English settlers born in Ireland, not Gaelic Irish. *(Mayflower descent would be a separate, notable finding; verify with the GSMD Silver Books before claiming it.)*

## 6. Henry Martin / Nancy "Storm" — Belfast street directories (NEGATIVE, with one pointer)

I grepped the Lennon Wylie transcriptions of the Belfast directories for 1819, 1824 (Pigot), 1843 (alphabetical and street lists) and 1852. They contain **no Storm** and **no Henry Martin** householder. The only near-spelling is **Stormont**: Wm. Stormont, 43 North Street (1824), and James Stormont, painter and glazier, 50 North Street (1843). That fits the s3 idea that "Storm" is a clipped Stormont. Stormont is itself a Scottish-origin surname (from the Stormont district of Perthshire), so it would be an Ulster-Scots thread. **SPECULATIVE**; only the 1862 marriage image can settle it.
