# Ulster origins of Henry "Harry" Martin (1830–1896), Sevastopol, Door County, WI

Research session 2026-10-08. Prior findings in `src/content/people/Martin/henry-martin.md` (read-only) are cited as "local note" and were not re-verified unless stated. **New this session:** the sister's identity (Sarah Ann Martin, m. John Kehoe 24 Oct 1881), her death in 1915, the officiant conflict over the 1862 marriage, and the Methodist identification of Rev. David Lewis.

Evidence grades used below: **CONFIRMED** = read directly in a primary or near-primary source this session. **PROBABLE** = strong but indirect. **SPECULATIVE** = hypothesis only.

Door County newspaper archive used: Door County Library / ResCarta, https://archive.co.door.wi.us (free, no login). I searched by phrase and pulled the OCR text through `/servlet/RcWebTextData?ext_obj_id=<id>&pg_idx=<n>`. Quotes below come from the OCR, with obvious OCR errors silently fixed. ## Q1. Is there an Irish baptism c.1830 for Henry Martin, son of Henry & Nancy/Ann (Storm), in the Belfast/Co. Down area?

### Takeaway
No baptism was found. FamilySearch's indexed historical records returned **zero** hits for a Henry Martin born in Ireland 1826–34 with father Henry and mother Nancy. However, the broad control searches also returned zero, so this result may reflect a search or rendering failure rather than a true absence (see Gaps). The only birthplace evidence is still the 1896 obituary: born "at or near the city of Belfast," 18 Aug 1830.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED:** Advocate obituary, 15 Aug 1896: born "at or near the city of Belfast, Ireland, August 18, 1830"; came to America in 1851; lived in New York state until fall 1854; at Cedar River, Mich., until spring 1857; arrived Sturgeon Bay July 1857. — Door County Advocate 1896-08-15 p1, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us) (transcribed in local note `henry-martin.md`)
- FamilySearch historical-records search, run in the browser with a logged-in session, all 0 results:
  - Henry Martin, father Henry Martin, mother Nancy, birth in Ireland 1826–1834. — [FamilySearch search](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/record/results?q.givenName=Henry&q.surname=Martin&q.fatherGivenName=Henry&q.fatherSurname=Martin&q.motherGivenName=Nancy&q.birthLikePlace=Ireland&q.birthLikeDate.from=1826&q.birthLikeDate.to=1834&count=50)
  - Control search: surname Martin, father Henry, any place Ireland 1820–1845. Zero results is implausible for such a common name, so treat all of these FS "0" results as **unreliable**. — [FamilySearch search](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/record/results?q.surname=Martin&q.fatherGivenName=Henry&q.anyPlace=Ireland&q.anyDate.from=1820&q.anyDate.to=1845&count=100)
- Prior work (local note): Co. Down church registers are not on free irishgenealogy.ie. The indexed Antrim/Down church registers are on RootsIreland (Ulster Historical Foundation, paywalled), and the originals are at PRONI. Irish civil birth registration starts only in 1864.

### Inferences
- **PROBABLE:** if any Irish record exists, it is a church register for a greater-Belfast parish. Candidates: Shankill (C of I), the Belfast Presbyterian congregations (Rosemary St, Fisherwick, etc.), or on the Down side Knockbreda/Ballymacarrett and Holywood. Most of these are indexed only on RootsIreland.
- Many Belfast Presbyterian baptismal registers for c.1830 are fragmentary or do not survive. A negative result would not disprove a Presbyterian origin.

### Gaps
- RootsIreland (paid login) was not searched. It is the single most important Irish index for this question.
- The FamilySearch result counts appear to have rendered as "0" even for very broad queries. Re-run them by hand in a normal browser session.
- PRONI's church-records guide and the Belfast congregation lists were not consulted this session.

## Q2. Is there a Martin–Storm marriage c.1820–1830 in Antrim/Down?

### Takeaway
None was found. The surname Storm is nearly invisible in Irish records of this period. The "Nancy Storm" identification rests entirely on a FamilySearch tree claim attributed to the 1862 Door County marriage record, and nobody has yet read that record directly.

### Cited Findings
- FamilySearch historical records, surname **Storm (exact)**, any place Ireland, 1780–1870: **0 results**. The same reliability caveat as Q1 applies. — [FamilySearch search](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/record/results?q.surname=Storm&q.surname.exact=on&q.anyPlace=Ireland&q.anyDate.from=1780&q.anyDate.to=1870&count=50)
- Prior work (local note, not re-verified):
  - Ros Davies' Co. Down surname database has no Storm entries; it jumps from Storey/Story to Stormonth.
  - PRONI's free name search (1740/1766 census fragments, 1775 petitions, pre-1858 will indexes) has no exact Storm, only Stormont(h)/Stormey, all in the Belfast area.
  - irishgenealogy.ie Co. Down civil records hold one exact Storm: the death of William Storm, 1887, Belfast district, age 63 (group reg. 6949333).
- **CONFIRMED (new): the 1862 marriage officiant differs between sources.**
  - The 1896 Advocate obituary names Judge M. E. Lyman, at the home of Joseph Lavassor (local note).
  - The Advocate's "Fifty Years Ago" column (14 Nov 1912) says: "Married, in Sturgeon Bay, Wis., November 12 1862, by Robert Graham, Esq., Mr. Henry Martin of Sevastopol and Mrs. Eliza Peters of Nasewaupee." — The Sturgeon Bay Advocate 1912-11-14 p2, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
  - The 1912 column was reprinted from the 1862 issue, so it is closer to the event than the obituary. It also shows Eliza was living in Nasewaupee, which matches the 1860 Martin Peters household.
  - Neither version names Henry's parents. The original register entry (WHS Door Vol 1 p 11) is still the only possible source for "Henry Martin Sr. & Nancy Storm."

### Inferences
- **SPECULATIVE:** "Storm" could be a clerk's or indexer's rendering of a different Ulster surname. The rarity of Storm in Ulster makes a misreading more likely than a true Storm family. Candidates: **Stormont/Stormonth** (Belfast-area, documented), **Storey/Story** (common in Antrim/Down), or less likely **Sturgeon** or **Stern**.
- If the 1862 record does say "Storm," a Belfast-urban family is more plausible than a rural Down family, given the single 1887 Belfast Storm death.

### Gaps
- The 1862 marriage register entry has not been viewed.
- No Irish marriage index (RootsIreland, or PRONI pre-1845 Presbyterian/C of I marriage registers) has been searched for Martin × Storm/Stormont/Storey.

## Q3. Where was the Storm surname found in Ulster, and is it Scottish or English?

### Takeaway
I could not get Griffith's Valuation counts. askaboutireland.ie and johngrenham.com both sit behind Cloudflare bot checks (HTTP 403 from scripts; the browser hit a verification interstitial, which I did not attempt to bypass). The available evidence points to Storm being a **rare, English-origin** name in Ulster. It is not a recognised Scottish-planter name.

### Cited Findings
- Storm is described as an Old English surname whose early records cluster in southern and eastern England (Essex, Kent, Sussex), and is rare in modern Britain (about 558 bearers). This is an algorithmic aggregator, so the evidence is weak. — [britishsurnames.uk/surname/storm](https://britishsurnames.uk/surname/storm)
- No source found associates Storm with Scottish settlement in Ulster. — search summary; see [Ulster Historical Foundation / Book of Ulster Surnames listing](https://genealogical.com/store/the-book-of-ulster-surnames/) (Storm entry not checked)
- askaboutireland.ie Griffith's name search for "Storm" returned 403 to scripts and a "Performing security verification" page in the browser. — [askaboutireland Griffith's search URL](https://www.askaboutireland.ie/griffith-valuation/index.xml?action=nameSearch&familyname=Storm)
- johngrenham.com surname pages also returned a 403 Cloudflare challenge. — https://www.johngrenham.com/findasurname.php?surname=Storm
- Prior work (local note): Ulster hits are almost all Stormont(h), a Scottish locational name from the Stormont district of Perthshire.

### Inferences
- **PROBABLE:** a genuine "Storm" in 1820s Belfast would be an English (or possibly Scandinavian/Dutch maritime) incomer, not a plantation-era Scots family. A **Stormont/Stormonth** reading would point instead to a Scottish-origin Belfast family.
- **Martin** is common in Antrim and Down among both Scots-Presbyterian and English-Anglican stock. The surname alone cannot settle Scots-Irish vs Anglo-Irish.

### Gaps
- No Griffith's or Tithe Applotment counts for Storm/Stormont in Antrim or Down. Retry askaboutireland in a normal browser (pass the Cloudflare check manually), or use the FamilySearch "Ireland, Griffith's Valuation" collection or Findmypast's Tithe Applotment index.

## Q4. Who was the sister, Mrs. John Keho(e)?

### Takeaway
**She was Sarah Ann Martin.** She married John Kehoe of Baileys Harbor on 24 Oct 1881, and the wedding notice calls her a sister of Henry Martin, the Sevastopol postmaster. She died at North Bay (town of Baileys Harbor) on Monday 8 Mar 1915, reported as "a lady of 60 years." That age makes her about 25 years younger than Henry, which is the most interesting new lead (see Inferences). She was John Kehoe's second wife; Kehoe children were born in Door County from 1865 to 1874.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED:** Advocate, 3 Nov 1881, Baileys Harbor column: "Mr. John Keho, of this town, and a Miss Martin, of Sevastopol, were married in this village last week. The bride is a sister of Henry Martin, Esq., the popular Sevastopol postmaster." — Door County Advocate 1881-11-03 p3, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- **CONFIRMED:** same issue, MARRIED column: "KEHOE—MARTIN.—At Bailey's Harbor, Wis., Oct. 24, 1881, by Wm. Jackson, Esq., Mr. John Kehoe and Miss Sarah Ann Martin." Wm. Jackson was a justice of the peace, so this was a civil ceremony. — Door County Advocate 1881-11-03 p3, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- **CONFIRMED:** WHS Pre-1907 Vital Records index: Kehoe, John, marriage, 24 Oct 1881, Door County, **Vol 1, p 153** (record MR2619003). The bride is not separately indexed. — [wisconsinhistory.org/record/vital/MR2619003/kehoe-john](https://www.wisconsinhistory.org/record/vital/MR2619003/kehoe-john)
- WHS Door County Kehoe births: Thomas (19 Nov 1865), John (30 Jun 1867, reel 51 #471), James (25 Feb 1869), William (10 Nov 1870), Sarah (5 Apr 1872, reel 51 #474), Michael (20 Mar 1874). There is also a Michael Kehoe marriage on 30 Jan 1882. — [WHS search "Kehoe", county Door](https://www.wisconsinhistory.org/global-content/global-search/?keyword=Kehoe&fq=county%3ADoor)
  - These births come before the 1881 marriage. Either John had an earlier wife or they belong to another Kehoe family (there were Kehoe/Keogh landholders in T30 R27–28). **PROBABLE:** Sarah Ann was a second wife and stepmother.
- **CONFIRMED:** Advocate, 15 Aug 1896: "Mrs. John Keho was called to Sevastopol last Saturday by the serious illness of her brother, Henry Martin … quite inconsolable at the loss of a favorite relative." — Door County Advocate 1896-08-15 p8, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- **CONFIRMED:** Advocate, 29 Nov 1902 (Sevastopol): "Mr. and Mrs. John Kehoe of Baileys Harbor, were the guests of Thos. Melville and Henry Martin … Mrs. Kehoe is an aunt of Mr. Martin and Mrs. Melville." — The Advocate 1902-11-29 p8, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
  - **New implication: Mrs. Thomas Melville was a biological child of Henry Sr.** She is a niece of Henry's sister, so not a Peters stepdaughter.
- **CONFIRMED:** Advocate, 6 Jan 1906 (Sevastopol): "Mr. and Mrs. John Keho of Baileys Harbor spent Christmas with the Martin Bros. The latter is a sister of the late Henry Martin." — The Advocate 1906-01-06 p8, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- Advocate, 12 Mar 1898: Mr. and Mrs. Thos. Melville visited Mr. and Mrs. John Keho at Baileys Harbor. — The Advocate 1898-03-12 p8, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- **CONFIRMED:** Door County News, 10 Mar 1915, DEATHS: "Mrs. John Kehoe of North Bay passed away at her home at that place on Monday. Mrs. Kehoe was a lady of 60 years and leaves a husband to mourn her sudden death." — Door County News 1915-03-10 p1, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- John Kehoe later records:
  - Moved in 1909 to a newly acquired home near Mud Bay. — The Advocate 1909-03-04 p1
  - Listed at R. 1, Ephraim in 1916. — Sturgeon Bay Advocate 1916-06-01 p3
  - Wintered near Gladstone, Mich., in 1887. — Door County Advocate 1887-11-19 p3
  - All at [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)

### Inferences
- **PROBABLE:** "a lady of 60 years" puts Sarah Ann's birth at about 1854–55. That is roughly 24 years after Henry, and close to or after his 1851 emigration. There are three readings:
  - (a) The age is a rough guess by the editor and she was really older. Note the item was a press-time notice.
  - (b) She was a much younger sister or half-sister, born in Ulster c.1855, who emigrated later. She was "of Sevastopol" in 1881, probably living with Henry.
  - (c) **SPECULATIVE:** she was born in America, which would mean Henry Sr. and Nancy also emigrated. That would put the parents (and their graves or records) in New York or Wisconsin.
  - Her 1900 and 1910 census entries (birth year, birthplace, immigration year) would decide among these.
- Her 1915 death falls after Wisconsin's statewide death registration began (Oct 1907). **Her death certificate should give her father's and mother's names and birthplaces.** This is the cheapest independent test of "Henry Martin & Nancy Storm."
- The 1881 marriage register (Door Vol 1 p 153) may also list the bride's parents and birthplace. Wisconsin marriage registers of the 1880s often have those columns.

### Gaps
- Her obituary beyond the brief notice has not been found. Searches for "Sarah Kehoe," "Mrs. Keho," and "Mrs. Jno. Kehoe" returned no other hits. The weekly Advocate of 11–13 Mar 1915 should be browsed page by page.
- Her burial place is unknown. Candidates: Baileys Harbor-area cemeteries; Bayside (Sturgeon Bay) beside Henry; or a Catholic cemetery if John Kehoe was Catholic. Kehoe is a Wexford/Leinster Catholic name, but they married before a JP.
- 1900/1910 census entries for John and Sarah Kehoe (Baileys Harbor) were not viewed. FamilySearch login is needed.

## Q5. Henry Martin's naturalization record

### Takeaway
Not found online. No Door County naturalization index entry for Henry Martin turned up in the newspaper archive or web searches. As postmaster (a federal appointee) and town treasurer, he was almost certainly a citizen, so a declaration and final papers should exist. He could have filed in New York (1851–54), Michigan (1854–57), or Door/Brown County after 1857.

### Cited Findings
- A newspaper archive search for "Henry Martin naturalization" found no relevant item. — [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- He is documented as Sevastopol postmaster for about 14 years and town treasurer for 15 successive terms. — Door County Democrat 1896-08-15 and Advocate 1896-08-15 obituaries (local note)

### Inferences
- **PROBABLE:** pre-1906 Door County naturalizations were filed in the Door County Circuit Court. In Wisconsin these county naturalization records are generally held by the WHS Area Research Center network; for the Door/Brown region that is UW–Green Bay. Pre-1906 forms seldom give a town of birth, but a declaration sometimes names the port and date of arrival.
- An 1850s declaration of intent could also be in New York (Niagara County, given Eliza's 1850 Lockport residence) or in Menominee County / Delta County, Michigan, for the Cedar River years.

### Gaps
- The holding repository was not verified this session.
- The postmaster appointment record (NARA M841, Record of Appointment of Postmasters, Door County) was not checked; it would date his tenure but not give his origin.

## Q6. Other Door County history and obituary text on his origins and religion

### Takeaway
No further origin detail has surfaced beyond "at or near Belfast." The funeral minister, **Rev. David Lewis, was the Methodist Episcopal pastor at Sturgeon Bay**. That is consistent with an Ulster Protestant (Methodist/Anglican-leaning) background, but it is not proof.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED:** Rev. David Lewis was a Methodist circuit rider who came to the Sturgeon Bay Methodist church as regular pastor in 1880, "served the M. E. churches in this part of Wisconsin for nearly 50 years," and died aged 92. A separate item describes him marrying a couple in 1885 as "Methodist pastor." — Door County Advocate (multiple issues; search "Rev. David Lewis"), [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- Henry's 1862 wedding (by a JP: Robert Graham or Judge Lyman) and his sister's 1881 wedding (by Wm. Jackson, Esq.) were both civil ceremonies. Neither tells us the family's church. — Advocate 1912-11-14 p2; Advocate 1881-11-03 p3, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)
- Later "Henry Martin of Sevastopol" items (1914–1922) are about **son** Henry Lincoln Martin and his family. That family used the Methodist parsonage for weddings, for example daughter Irene's wedding. — Door County Advocate (undated hit) / Door County News 1915-10-27 p6, [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)

### Inferences
- **SPECULATIVE, leaning Anglo-Irish / Church of Ireland or Methodist:** the pieces are a Methodist funeral, the next generation's Methodist weddings, civil marriages, and a mother's surname that is (if correct) English rather than Scottish.
  - Irish Methodism in 1830 was still largely a society within the Church of Ireland. Ulster Methodists were overwhelmingly of Anglican, not Presbyterian, background.
  - Against this: in 1890s Sturgeon Bay, the Methodist pastor may simply have been the available Protestant minister, and there was a Presbyterian presence in the county.
  - **Overall: Scots-Irish Presbyterian vs Anglo-Irish Church of Ireland cannot be decided on current evidence.** Slight lean to a Church of Ireland/Methodist milieu. Confidence is low.

### Gaps
- No county history (Holand's *History of Door County*, 1917; the 1881 *History of Northern Wisconsin*) was checked this session for a Henry Martin biography.
- The 1896 Advocate's follow-up issues (probate notice, possibly Sept 1896) were not read.

## Summary: CONFIRMED / PROBABLE / SPECULATIVE

**CONFIRMED**
- Henry was born at or near Belfast on 18 Aug 1830 (1896 obituary).
- His sister **Sarah Ann Martin** married **John Kehoe** at Baileys Harbor on **24 Oct 1881** (WHS Door Vol 1 p 153).
- Mrs. John Kehoe died at North Bay on Monday **8 Mar 1915**, reported as aged 60.
- Mrs. Kehoe was the aunt of Henry Martin (jr.) and of Mrs. Thomas Melville (1902), so Mrs. Melville was Henry's own daughter.
- The 1862 marriage officiant is given as Robert Graham, Esq. (1912 reprint) vs Judge M. E. Lyman (1896 obituary).
- The funeral minister, Rev. David Lewis, was Methodist Episcopal.

**PROBABLE**
- Sarah Ann was born c.1854–55 (if the reported age is right) and was John Kehoe's second wife.
- Henry's origin parish is in greater Belfast, on either the Antrim or the Down side.
- "Storm" is either a rare English-origin name or a misreading of Stormont/Storey.

**SPECULATIVE**
- The parents (or a widowed parent) emigrated after Henry, which would explain a sister c.25 years younger. This is unproven.
- The family background leans Church of Ireland/Methodist rather than Presbyterian. Confidence is low.
