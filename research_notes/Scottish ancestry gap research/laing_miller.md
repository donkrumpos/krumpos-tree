# Margaret Laing (KH36-ZYD) & William Miller (LZF4-CX5): parentage, origins, and the Canada link to Sophia Miller (MCXT-PV1)

Researched 2026-10-08. FamilySearch record pages were read while signed in through the user's browser. FamilySearch record/tree URLs below **require a free FamilySearch login** to view. Evidence labels: **CONFIRMED** = I saw the indexed record myself (URL given). **PROBABLE** = strong inference. **SPECULATIVE** = hypothesis only.

**Bottom line up front:** The FamilySearch couple "William Miller & Margaret Laing" is a **Perth/Rhynd, Scotland family whose children were baptised in Scotland from 1804 to 1823**. Sophia Miller (MCXT-PV1), who was born in **Canada c.1802**, does not fit into that family. No record connects her to them. The parent link Sophia → William Miller/Margaret Laing is **unsupported and very likely wrong**. The Newcastle 1799 marriage (to a "Margery Laing") may be a third, separate couple merged into the same profile. Do not extend Don's line through Laing/Miller until Sophia's real parents are found in Upper Canada.

## Q1. Is there a Rhynd OPR baptism c.1780–86 for Margaret Laing, and who are her parents?

### Takeaway
The FamilySearch index has no Margaret Laing born in Rhynd c.1780–86. The only Rhynd Margaret Laing in the index was born **10 Oct 1776**, daughter of **Charles Laing and Grizzel Menzies**. That makes her a plausible candidate for the Perth-family wife, but not a proven one. The profile's "abt 1783 of Rhynd" has **0 sources**. It appears to be derived from her children's Rhynd baptisms, not from a record of her own birth.

### Inferences
- PROBABLE: "of Rhynd" on KH36-ZYD comes from her children's Rhynd entries (Jean 1804, Margaret 1807; see Q3), not from her own baptism.
- SPECULATIVE: If the Perth-family wife was born in Rhynd, Margaret b. 1776 to Charles Laing & Grizzel Menzies is the only indexed candidate. Her last indexed child (William, 1823) would then have been born when she was about 47. That is unlikely but not impossible. If she was born c.1779–1783, she may be one of the Perth-burgh Margarets (William Laing & Margaret Buist, 1779), or her baptism may be unindexed. The FS note "1778" and the 1783 estimate both fit a birth in the late 1770s.
- The FamilySearch index is not complete for Rhynd. A ScotlandsPeople OPR search of Rhynd parish is needed to rule out an unindexed 1778–1786 entry.

### Gaps
- No death or burial record found for Margaret Laing / Miller. A Perth death after 1855 would name her parents; I did not search for one.
- The Charles Laing & Grizzel Menzies marriage and their other children were not found in the FS index.

## Q2. What does the St Nicholas, Newcastle, 4 Sep 1799 marriage record actually say?

### Takeaway
All three indexes give only names, date and church: **William Miller and *Margery* Laing, 4 Sep 1799, St Nicholas, Newcastle upon Tyne**. No ages, residences, witnesses or banns are indexed. Nothing in the index ties this couple to Perthshire or to Canada. The local note's "FamilySearch source 2:3KT62RR" is just the collection's internal "Source Details" code. The usable record link is ark 1:1:N6GN-L6F / N6GN-L6N.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED: "England Marriages, 1538–1973": William Miller and Margery Laing, 4 Sep 1799, Newcastle Upon Tyne, St Nicholas. Source Details "2:3KT62RR". Image unavailable. — [FS N6GN-L6F](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:N6GN-L6F) (citation ark [N6GN-L6N](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:N6GN-L6N))
- CONFIRMED: "England, Northumberland, Parish Registers, 1538-1950": William Miller & Margery Laing, marriage 4 Sep 1799, St Nicholas, Newcastle upon Tyne. No other fields indexed. The page says "Check Image Availability", so an image may be viewable to logged-in users or at an affiliate library. — [FS QPQ1-D1J9](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QPQ1-D1J9)
- CONFIRMED: Margaret's profile has four separate Northumberland Parish Register index entries for this 1799 event, plus the England Marriages entry. William's profile also has a "William **Millet**" Northumberland entry attached. These may be a banns entry, a duplicate register or bishop's transcript, or a mis-transcription. All were attached on 16 Jun 2019 by user "Carolynn Walker". — [FS sources KH36-ZYD](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/sources/KH36-ZYD); [FS LZF4-CX5](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/details/LZF4-CX5)
- CONFIRMED: A full St Nicholas marriage transcription for 1574–1812 was printed as Durham & Northumberland Parish Register Society vol. 28 (1913, ed. H. M. Wood). The Northumberland & Durham FHS sells the 1813–1839 continuation. — [NDFHS listing](https://ndfhs.org.uk/product/newcastle-st-nicholas-baptisms-marriages-and-burials-1813-1839/) (search snippet; volume not viewed)

### Inferences
- PROBABLE: The Newcastle couple was linked to the Perth couple only because the names match. Both "William Miller" and "Laing" are very common. Nothing in the index (residence, "of the parish of …", "a soldier", etc.) supports the link. The bride is indexed as **Margery**, while every Scottish record says Margaret/Margret. That is not decisive (the names were used interchangeably), but it is a flag.
- **Age plausibility:** The tree's William "1786" has 0 sources. His Collaborate notes list conflicting legacy birth years of **1774, 1779 and 1786** (all 2013, "UnknownMMMC-YWSB"), and his profile says "abt 1786 of Perth". A 13-year-old groom in 1799 is impossible under English law (Hardwicke's Act required parental consent under 21, and the canonical minimum was 14). So 1786 must be wrong if the 1799 marriage is his. The 1774/1779 dates would make him 20–25 at marriage, which is plausible. A bride born 1776 would be ~23 at the marriage, which is plausible. A bride born 1783 would be ~16, which is possible with consent but unusual. — [FS collaborate LZF4-CX5](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/collaborate/LZF4-CX5)
- SPECULATIVE: A Perth weaver or soldier marrying in Newcastle in 1799, during the French Revolutionary War with Scottish fencible and militia units moving around, and then returning to Perth, is conceivable. However, no evidence for it was found.

### Gaps
- The register image was not viewed. Pre-1813 St Nicholas entries sometimes give "of this parish", the groom's occupation (e.g., "soldier in the … Regt") and witnesses. Viewing the image or the DNPRS vol. 28 transcription is the single best test of the Newcastle-to-Perth link.
- FreeREG and Durham Records Online were not checked: FreeREG search is form-driven and Durham Records Online is paywalled.

## Q3. Evidence for William & Margaret in Canada 1800–1830, or elsewhere

### Takeaway
None was found. The indexed records show a William Miller/Millar & Margaret Laing family **continuously in Scotland (Rhynd and Perth) from 1804 to 1823**, with children dying in Perth in 1872 and 1874. Sophia (MCXT-PV1) was born **in Canada c.1802**, according to the 1850 census. She cannot be shown to belong to this family and almost certainly doesn't.

### Inferences
- PROBABLE (strong): Sophia is **wrongly attached** to William Miller & Margaret Laing. Four reasons:
  1. She was born in **Canada** (1850 census, and per local notes her daughter Eliza's 1880 census), but the couple was baptising children in Rhynd in Nov 1804 and Apr 1807 and in Perth through 1823.
  2. Her census age gives b. ~1802, which collides with the Campsie 1802 "Margaret" slot and the 1803–04 Jean/Jane.
  3. Neither of her two sources names her parents.
  4. The profile already mixes at least two and probably three couples: Campsie 1802 (Millar/Lang), Newcastle 1799 (Miller/Margery Laing) and Rhynd/Perth 1804–1823 (Miller/Laing). This is the classic "same-name merge" pattern.
- PROBABLE: The real Perth family is William Miller(/Millar) and Margaret Laing of Rhynd, later Perth (children 1804–1823). Their son Robert was a Perth grocer. Their children's 1855+ death records (Jane 1872, Robert 1874, and the others) are the best route to William's occupation and Margaret's maiden-name confirmation.
- SPECULATIVE: The real Sophia Miller (b. c.1802, Upper Canada) is more likely the daughter of a **Niagara-region or Upper Canada Miller family**: Loyalist, American "late Loyalist" or British settler. Her later life in Lockport, NY, just across the Niagara River, and a c.1829 marriage in Ontario both point that way. The 10-year age gap with an English-born husband (b. ~1812; the tree's "1808" is unsourced) is also worth noting.
- SPECULATIVE: The soldier hypothesis (Newcastle 1799 to Canada c.1802) has no supporting evidence. It only becomes worth testing if the Newcastle register image shows the groom was a soldier.

### Gaps
- Upper Canada Land Petitions, Niagara District records, Loyalist lists and the 1825 Lower Canada census were not searched for William Miller. That search is not useful until Sophia's parents are re-established independently, because the name is far too common.
- Thomas Hutchison & Sophia Miller's Upper Canada marriage record (c.1829) was not found in this pass. It is the most likely record to name her father or residence.
- Eliza Hutchinson's 1880 census (mother b. Canada) is cited from local notes. I did not re-verify it.
