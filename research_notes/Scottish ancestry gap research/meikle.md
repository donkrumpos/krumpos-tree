# Meikle / "Mielke" family of Sturgeon Bay: does Fred Meikle descend from William Meikle (1813–1893)?

Research session 2026-10-08. Main source: the Door County Library digitized newspaper archive (archive.co.door.wi.us, free, ResCarta). I read the OCR text directly through its `/servlet/RcWebTextData` endpoint. For the decisive 1914 obituary I also pulled the page image and read it, because the OCR was garbled. The search-result URLs for that archive are session-bound, so each citation gives the archive's document ID (`ext_obj_id`). To reopen a page, go to `https://archive.co.door.wi.us/jsp/RcWebImageViewer.jsp?doc_id=<ID>` or search the quoted phrase at https://archive.co.door.wi.us.

**Headline:** Fred is almost certainly **NOT** a son of William Meikle. Alexander Meikle's 1914 obituary lists all his surviving brothers, and Fred is not among them, even though Fred was alive and living locally at the time. Separately, Fred's marriage date and his children's names point toward a different family, possibly a German Mielke/Michel family that was later anglicized. The earlier finding "RESOLVED SCOTTISH" in `emil-mielke.md` should be reopened.

Evidence grades: **CONFIRMED** means I saw the record or page. **PROBABLE** means strongly indicated but no direct record. **SPECULATIVE** means a hypothesis only.

## 1. Is Fred Meikle a son of William Meikle? (Fred → William)

### Takeaway
**DISPROVEN, with high confidence (not absolute).** Alexander Meikle, William's eldest son, died in April 1914. His obituary names four surviving brothers: James, John, Dan and Thomas. It also names one sister, Mrs. McBride. Fred is not listed, yet Fred was alive and living in or near Sturgeon Bay from 1893 to 1917. Fred's 40th wedding anniversary in March 1911 dates his marriage to about March 1871. That date, and his children's names, also fit poorly with the Scottish family.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED (I read the page image myself).** Door County Democrat, 10 Apr 1914, p. 1, "Death of Alex Meikle": "Mr. Meikle is survived by four brothers, James and John of Sturgeon Bay town, Dan of Spokane Bridge, Washington, and Thomas, who is also a resident of the west, and one sister, Mrs. McBride, of Milton, N. D." Doc ID `0a8b94b5-5745-457c-a604-522158934fc2/wsbd0000/20120718/00001109`, page index 0 — [archive.co.door.wi.us](https://archive.co.door.wi.us). Search phrase: "Alexander Meikle".
- **CONFIRMED.** The same obituary says Alexander was born **18 Nov 1839 in "Stirlingshire," Scotland**, not Glasgow. It says he "when ten years of age came to America" (about 1849–50), came to Door County in 1866, and married **Jeanette R. Robinson on 2 May 1867**. One daughter survived him: Mrs. F. B. Jennerjahn. He had been a justice of the peace in Gardner (late 1860s), school district clerk in 1874–75, and a Third Ward alderman and supervisor. He was buried at Bayside. Same document as above.
- **CONFIRMED.** Fred was alive and local when Alexander died. Door County Democrat, 6 Mar 1917, "Sawyer Short Notes": "Mr. and Mrs. Fred Meikle went to Green Bay Wednesday." Doc ID `0a8b94b5-…/20120718/00001261`, page index 1 — [archive](https://archive.co.door.wi.us). Sawyer is the west side of Sturgeon Bay, so a brother there could not have been overlooked as living far away.
- **CONFIRMED.** Door County Democrat, 10 Mar 1911, "Town of Sturgeon Bay" column: "Saturday evening a large number of people both old and young, gathered at the home of Mr. and Mrs. Fred Meikel to celebrate the **fortieth anniversary of their wedding**." The same column has "Mrs. Kloudy and Ella Meikel called on Mrs. Andrew Johnson…" Doc ID `0a8b94b5-…/20120718/00000948`, page index 4. **This puts the marriage at about March 1871**, not 1875–81 as previously assumed. That explains why earlier searches of the 1875–81 Wisconsin marriage index found nothing.
- **CONFIRMED.** Door County Democrat, 3 Mar 1911, "Town of Sturgeon Bay": "Mrs. Fred Meikle is quite sick… Misses Anna Johnson, Ella and Bertha Meikel and Julius Melkel [sic] drove to Forestville Saturday." The same column also has "Miss Ella Mickles and sister, Mrs. Kloudy, have returned from Chicago." The school-district roll in that column (Dist. No. 5) includes Kohlmorgen, Quam, Robertson and Johnson children. Doc ID `0a8b94b5-…/20120718/00000947`, page index 4.
- **CONFIRMED.** The Democrat, 4 May 1893, county correspondence from **TORNADO** (southern Door County), dated 27 April: "Frank Rayman and Fred Meikle have about completed their new saloon in this place." Doc ID `7f27568d-68b1-4968-bf42-eda452e4b5f4/wsbd0000/20120522/00000883`, page index 3. **This is the same issue as William Meikle's obituary.** That obituary calls William only "father of Alex Meikle, of this city" and says nothing of Fred.
- **CONFIRMED (correction to the local notes).** The 1899 "valuable horse, owned by Fred Meikle, died" item (Door County Democrat, 21 May 1899, doc `0a8b94b5-…/20120718/00000330`, page index 7) does not appear to be from a Fish Creek or Egg Harbor column. The neighbouring items concern the Gardner post office, the "Taube district" school, the Cheeseville schoolhouse and Clay Banks cemetery, which places it in southern or west-side Door County. The OCR of the column heading is unreadable, so I could not identify the exact column.
- **CONFIRMED.** William Meikle's FindAGrave memorial links only Alexander, James and John as his children. It gives no spouse or parents. Ancestry record hints attached to it (an 1850 US census, an 1855 New York state census, and Scottish OPR marriage and baptism indexes) are unverified — [FindAGrave #130602798](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/130602798).

### Inferences
- Obituaries in this paper routinely listed all surviving siblings. Alexander's lists five, including a brother in Washington state and a sister in North Dakota. Omitting a living brother in Sawyer is very unlikely. **The Fred → William link should be downgraded from "probable" to "unlikely/disproven."** One remaining possibility is that Fred was a nephew (for example, a son of a William Meikle relative). I found no evidence for this.
- Fred's children named in records are Emil (b. 1881), Ernest, Oscar, Iva, and very probably **Ella, Bertha and Julius** (Meikel, Town of Sturgeon Bay, 1911), plus a married daughter, **Mrs. Kloudy**. These are German-pattern names. Together with a marriage around 1871, they make it plausible (SPECULATIVE) that Fred was a German or German-Lutheran immigrant named **Mielke, Michel, Mickel or Meikel**, whose surname was later written the Scottish way in English-language records. The family's own variants (Meikel, Mickles, Melkel, Meikle, Mielke, Miekle) fit this spelling drift.
- The 1911 Meikel family lived in the **Town of Sturgeon Bay** in a school district with the Kohlmorgen family. In 1956, a "William Mikels" died whose siblings were Mrs. Ida Kohlmorgan, Mrs. C. J. Kumm, Fred Mikels and George Mikels (Door County Advocate, 21 Feb 1956, card of thanks, doc `1e8fc801-90a4-4104-8e86-19a1ea0947dc/wsbd0000/20141103/00001127`). The same issue has an estate notice for "Alexander Meikle," late of the Town of Sturgeon Bay, with Ida Meikle as administratrix. **At least one other Meikle/Mikels family lived in the town.** It must be kept separate from both William's Scots family and Fred's family.
- **Previous finding I have NOT verified:** the earlier note that "Fred Meikle" was in the Fish Creek or Egg Harbor area in 1899. The column context suggests otherwise.

### Gaps
- I did not see a census entry for Fred himself (1880, 1900 or 1910). FamilySearch census indexes require a free login, which I did not have. That is the one record that would give his birthplace and his parents' birthplaces.
- I did not read James Meikle's 1917 obituary (Door County Democrat, 22 Jun 1917, p. 1). It should list brothers as a second check.
- I could not reconstruct the 1899 column heading because the OCR is garbled.

## 2. William Meikle: Scottish parish, parents, wife

### Takeaway
William's obituary gives only "near Glasgow" (b. 6 Feb 1813). His son Alexander's obituary gives Alexander's birthplace as **Stirlingshire** (1839). So the family's parish of origin is more likely in Stirlingshire, or on the Stirlingshire–Lanarkshire border, than in Glasgow city. His wife's name was not found in any record I could access.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** The Democrat, 4 May 1893, p. 1, "OBITUARY MENTION — Wm. Meikle Died Only a Few Days After His Arrival In This City": born "near Glasgow, Scotland, Feb. 6, 1813". The obituary says that "In 1848 he came to this country and settled in the state of New York, where he resided until 1856, when together with his family he moved to Toronto, Canada. The last eight years of his life were spent at Chelsey [Chesley], Ontario." It says he died 1 May 1893 at Alex's home, nine days after arriving, and was a Presbyterian. Doc ID `7f27568d-…/20120522/00000883`, page index 0. *Correction:* the move was to **Toronto in 1856**, then to Chesley about 1885. Earlier notes said "NY then Ontario — Chesley".
- **CONFIRMED.** Alexander was born in Stirlingshire on 18 Nov 1839 (1914 obituary, above). He came to America at about age ten, which is consistent with an 1848–49 emigration.
- **CONFIRMED.** William's surviving children in 1914 were Alexander (d. 1914), **James**, **John**, **Dan** (Spokane Bridge, Washington), **Thomas** ("of the west"), and a daughter, **Mrs. McBride** of Milton, North Dakota (1914 obituary, above).
- **CONFIRMED (index hints only, not opened).** FindAGrave attaches Ancestry hints for William to the 1850 US census, the 1855 New York state census, "Scotland, Select Marriages" and "Church of Scotland Marriages 1615–1854" — [FindAGrave #130602798](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/130602798). Ancestry requires a subscription, so I could not see which entries these are.
- **Negative result.** A web search found a different William Meikle (b. 1824 Glasgow, d. 1896 Lachute, Quebec), who is **not** ours — [rootsweb johnmeikle-maryhoatson](https://freepages.rootsweb.com/~cdobie/genealogy/johnmeikle-maryhoatson.htm). The HGIS Canada 1881 Chesley list I checked showed no Meikle on the page that loaded — [HGIS Chesley 1881](https://jimclifford.ca/hgiscanada/places/on/chesley-t-v-on177010-1881/residents/). The page was a partial list, so this is not conclusive.

### Inferences
- PROBABLE: William married about 1838. Scottish OPR (Old Parish Register) baptisms of his children should be searched in **Stirlingshire parishes** first, especially those near Glasgow such as Campsie, Baldernock, Strathblane, Kilsyth, Denny, Falkirk and St Ninians. Lanarkshire should be searched second. The children to look for are Alexander (1839), James (about 1841), John (about 1845), and probably Dan/Daniel and Thomas (about 1843–48).
- PROBABLE: the family should appear in the 1850 US census in New York state, and in the 1861 and 1871 Canada censuses in or near **Toronto/York County**, not in Bruce County. They should appear in Bruce County (Chesley, Elderslie/Sullivan townships) only in 1891.

### Gaps
- Wife's name: not found. William's 1893 obituary does not name her, which suggests she died before 1893.
- I did not consult ScotlandsPeople (pay-per-view, login required) or the FamilySearch OPR indexes (login required).
- I did not obtain the 1861, 1871, 1881 or 1891 Canada census entries. Library and Archives Canada and automatedgenealogy.com were not reached in this session.

## 3. Mary (Johnsen) Meikle: origin, parents, marriage

### Takeaway
I found no record of Mary's birthplace or parents. The newly found 40th-anniversary notice moves the marriage to **about March 1871**. That is the key new search parameter.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** Married about March 1871 (40th anniversary celebrated early March 1911) — Door County Democrat, 10 Mar 1911, doc `0a8b94b5-…/20120718/00000948`.
- **CONFIRMED.** "Mrs. Fred Meikle is quite sick" — 3 Mar 1911, doc `…/00000947`.
- **CONFIRMED (negative).** Every "Mary Meikle" hit in the archive (1918-05-31, 1918-06-14, 1930-10-31, 1970-04-21) was checked:
  - The 1918 hits are tax-sale legal notices that name a Mary Meikle as a property holder in Ward One, Subdivision 5, City of Sturgeon Bay.
  - The 1930 hit is a different, younger Mary Meikle of Sevastopol, a niece of the Brauer family.
  - The 1970 hit is Emil's obituary.
  - None is Mary's obituary. Doc IDs `…/00001325`, `…/00001327`, `1e8fc801-…/20131118/00000299`, `1e8fc801-…/20151119/00001075`.
- **Previous finding (local notes), not re-verified:** the "Johnsen" maiden name comes from FindAGrave #126311941 (Emil's memorial).

### Inferences
- SPECULATIVE: a marriage in 1871 means Fred and Mary married before Wisconsin's marriage registration was reliable. They may also have married outside Door County, possibly in Germany, Norway or Denmark before emigrating, or in Kewaunee, Brown or Manitowoc county. If Fred was German, "Johnsen" may be a Low German or Danish (Schleswig) form rather than Norwegian.

### Gaps
- Mary's birthplace, parents and death date are all unknown. Census entries from 1880, 1900 and 1910 would give her birthplace and year of immigration.

## 4. Published histories and other Meikle items

### Takeaway
The newspaper archive was more productive than any published history. The 1914 Alexander obituary is the most important single document found. I did not reach Holand's *History of Door County* (1917) or Wisconsin Necrology.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** Probate of Alexander Meikle's will: Jeanette Jennerjahn named executrix; claims deadline 12 Nov 1914 — Door County Advocate, 28 May 1914, doc `b906834c-d092-4fa8-a27e-15e1b2759076/wsbd0000/20130111/00000962`.
- **CONFIRMED.** "Frederick Meikle" and "Jacob Meikle" put up playground equipment at Peninsular Center school in 1950 — Door County Advocate, 26 Sep 1950, doc `1e8fc801-…/20141103/00000566`. This is a later generation, probably of the Sevastopol/Jacksonport-area Meikle family, and not our Fred.
- **CONFIRMED.** "Fred Mielke and son Frederick Jr." appear in Sturgeon Bay in 1948 — Door County Advocate, 8 Jun 1948, doc `1e8fc801-…/20141103/00000337`. This is a 20th-century Mielke family and unlikely to be ours.

The unread dates are 1876-05-26, 1894-12-01, 1897-05-29, 1898-01-08, 1898-05-14, 1903-05-02, 1903-10-24, 1907-06-01, 1907-08-03, 1907-11-16, 1910-11-11, 1911-01-27, 1912-03-14, the 1914 issues (Feb–Nov), 1917-12-28, 1919-11-28, 1920-08-13, 1920-12-10, 1923-04-05, 1924-01-30, 1924-02-14, 1926-02-11, the 1927–1933 issues, 1938-06-24 and 1944 — [archive](https://archive.co.door.wi.us). **The 1919–1926 hits are prime candidates for Fred's and Mary's death notices.**
