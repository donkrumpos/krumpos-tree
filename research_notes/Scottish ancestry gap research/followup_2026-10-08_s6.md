PROBABLE that it is our Thomas.** Same church as the 1831 baptism, and the timing fits: Rhoda was born in Canada c.1845, and the family was at Lockport by 1850. A household of four could be Thomas, Sophia, Rhoda and Mary (aged c.17). This moves the family's departure from Canada to **1848–50** and puts them in the *town* of Niagara, not the township.

## Lead 1 — William & Margery Miller of Niagara (fl. 1792–1801)

### (a) St Mark's register (OHS *Papers and Records* vol. 3, 1901; archive.org `papersrecordsontv3onta`, re-checked against a second scan `earlyrecordsofst00carn_0`)
- **Baptisms, 1792:** the first batch of Addison's register (9–22 Jul 1792) includes "Margaret Miller, of William and Margery" (15 Jul 1792), beside Loyalist township families (McNabb, Slingerland, Silverthorn, Butler). The batch includes older children, so Margaret's birth year is not known.
- **1799:** "James Miller, of William and Margery."
- **19 Apr 1801:** "Sophia Miller, of William and Margery" and "Louisa Miller, of William and Margery", bracketed with a marginal word that OCRs as "™ • … j wms'" and "\ m •", **= "twins"**. **CONFIRMED (transcript).**
- **Burials, 1792–1829 (vol. 3 pp. 66–73):** the only Miller burial is "Looisa Miller, infant", 25 Sep 1801. **NEGATIVE for any William or Margery Miller burial at St Mark's to 1829.** The book's burials stop in 1829, apart from a few added for 1850.
- **No St Mark's marriage** of a William Miller to a Margery (Addison's marriages begin in 1792, after Margaret's birth). NEGATIVE.

### (b) Loyalist / first-settler lists — NEGATIVE
- **Old UE List** (Crown Lands copy, Appendix B of *The Centennial of the Settlement of Upper Canada by the United Empire Loyalists, 1784–1884*, Toronto 1885; archive.org `centennialofsett00unituoft`): Millers listed are Andrew, Cornelius, Garret, George, Gilbert, John, Jacob, James, Jonathan, Nicholas, Peter, Stephen, Thomas and Zebeda. **No William Miller.**
- **Niagara census of 25 Aug 1782 and 1783 return** (as printed in *Names Only But Much More*, 1915): the only Miller is **Peter Miller**. No William.
- **Inference (SPECULATIVE):** William was probably not a recognised Loyalist or a Butler's Ranger. He was more likely a soldier of the Niagara garrison, a discharged soldier, or a post-1783 British settler. That is consistent with a Scottish or Irish origin, but nothing names it.

### (c) William Duff Miller — probably a different man (NEGATIVE as Sophia's father, on present evidence)
- St Andrew's Presbyterian: "Messrs. Alick Gardner and William Miller" took pew 16 from 1 Oct 1802, with "Half 16, Wm. Miller" in Sept 1803. "Wm. Miller" was present at the meeting at Knox's Tavern, 7 Jan 1804, and on a subscription list. "Mr. William Miller" was the secretary of the rebuilding committee in June 1819 (*Gleaner*, 17 Jun 1819). Sources: *Names Only But Much More* (NHS 27–28); *Records of Niagara* (NHS 17–25 vol.).
- **W. D. Miller** (Lt.-Col. William Duff Miller): "**Born in 1786 of Scottish parentage**, nearly his whole life was spent in Canada". He died at Niagara on 18 Feb 1859, aged 73, "a resident of the frontier about 57 years and of the town upwards of 50 years". He lived first at Chippawa, where his eldest daughter was born in 1809. He was an elder of St Andrew's for 50 years and Deputy Clerk of the Crown. He married Ann Vansickle. Source: NHS publication no. 14 (archive.org `niagarahistorica0014unse`, pp. 41–42); Historic Niagara index entries 385681, 376714 and 385714 (https://historicniagara.ca/s/all/item/385681).
- **Assessment:** W. D. Miller reached the frontier c.1802 at about 16, so he is almost certainly the 1802–04 and 1819 St Andrew's "William Miller". He cannot be the William who was baptising children at St Mark's from 1792. **His "Scottish parentage" is not evidence for our William**, and no record links the two. SPECULATIVE only: a kinsman (for example a nephew sent out to join an uncle) is possible but completely unsupported.

### (d) FamilySearch full text, Lincoln and Niagara counties (Ontario), all record types
Method: the FS full-text search service, filtered to Canada › Ontario › {Lincoln, Niagara}. Every result page was read (up to 2,500 per query) and the OCR text was searched for the name pattern.
- "Sophia Hutchinson" (Sophia … Hutch\* or Hutch\* … Sophia): **1,998 documents, 0 matches. NEGATIVE.**
- "Margery Miller": 10,024 documents; the only Margery Millers are the 1890s Elizabeth Margery Miller (daughter of Robert and Deborah M. Miller) and a Woodington deed. Before 1865 there are only Margaret Millers (the Conrad Miller family of Gainsborough, 1860; a Caistor will, 1852). **NEGATIVE.**
- "Sophia Miller": 11,079 documents. Before 1870 there are only a Gainsborough deed index (Sophia and Richard Miller, 1866–71) and a Lincoln will of 1813–14 that names a married daughter "Sophia Miller". That woman was already married in 1813, so she is not ours (b. 1801). **NEGATIVE.**
- "Thomas Hutchinson" (first 2,500 of 36,013 documents; 212 pattern hits): before 1870 the only Thomas Hutchinson is in **St Catharines**. By the will of **Armstrong Hutchinson** of the Town of St Catharines, dated 9 Oct 1846, a St Paul Street town lot went to "Joseph Hutchinson, Thomas Hutchinson and Jane Hutchinson **the heirs of Edward and Jane** [Hutchinson] … to be equally divided between them **when the youngest becomes of age**" (deed memorials 1848, 1861–66). There are also quit claims of 1861–62 by Thomas Hutchinson. **NEGATIVE for our Thomas**: this Thomas was a minor in 1846, whereas ours was born c.1812 and a father by 1829. SPECULATIVE only: Edward, Jane and Armstrong could be English Hutchinson kin of the right generation in the same district. Nothing links them.
- Caveat: FS full text for Lincoln and Niagara is mostly post-1850 deeds and abstract books. Coverage of 1795–1840 wills and land papers is thin, so these negatives are weak.

## Lead 5 — Mary (Lavassor) Haines — CLOSED
- ***Weekly Expositor Independent*, Sturgeon Bay, 20 Nov 1885, p. 3, "DEATH OF MRS. HAINES"** (read from the image; Door archive, Weekly Expositor Independent vol. 13 no. 8). "Died — in this city, Nov. 14, 1885, of consumption, **Mary, daughter of the late Mr. and Mrs. Joseph Lavassor, and wife of Tallief [?] Haines, aged 26.** Mrs. Haines was born in this city, and lived here most of her lifetime … The **sudden death of her mother** … nearly two years since, so preyed upon her mind that from that time her health gradually declined … it was a great trial to her to think of leaving her husband and **two little girls, the elder only three and the younger one year old** … the dutiful daughter, the loving wife and mother, the dear sister … Her funeral services were on the 18th inst. … a long procession followed her to the cemetery." **CONFIRMED.**
