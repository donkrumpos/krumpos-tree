## Headline

1. **Fred Meikle was Swiss: Frederick Mägli / Magli / Magle, born in Switzerland c.1848.** The surname "Meikle" is an Americanised form of the Bernese name Mägli. It is not Scottish and not German. His naturalization image, the 1874 marriage index, and the 1905 and 1910 censuses all say Switzerland. The 1880 ("Bavaria") and 1900 ("Germany") census entries were wrong.
2. **Henry Martin administered the estates of both Joseph and Mary (Hutchinson) Lavassor in 1884**, at the request of their son Charles. This strengthens the Mary-as-Eliza's-sister case, but the file never states the relationship.
3. Leads 2–5 found nothing new (details below). The *A H Schultz* passenger candidate is ruled out on date.

### Joseph Lavassor estate — CONFIRMED (images)
Same film, **item 5** (88 images; group M9N8-B3J). In the **Petition for Letters, img 2** (ark 3:1:3QS7-99GZ-S9DX-6), Charles Lavassor says Joseph died intestate at Sturgeon Bay on **8 Mar 1883**, leaving widow Mary (now deceased) and the children. He asks that administration go to **Henry Martin of the Town of Sevastopol**. Img 7 is the special-administrator petition; Joseph's homestead had also burned. Img 10 is the special-administrator bond of 26 Feb 1884 (ark 3:1:3QS7-99GZ-S96B-G), with the same sureties. Again, no relationship is stated.

### Assessment
- **PROBABLE, strengthened (circumstantial):** Mary (Hutchinson) Lavassor was Eliza's sister. When both Lavassors were dead, their son asked for **Henry Martin, Eliza's husband**, rather than a Lavassor, Hutchinson or local man of business. This came 22 years after Henry and Eliza married in the Lavassor house. A "John Walker" witnessed the bond and worked Mary's farm in 1884, and he is very likely Eliza's son John Walker (b. c.1857), Mary's nephew. Neither file states a relationship.
- Mary's birthplace, parents and siblings are **not** in either file. Because her children survived her, Eliza could never have appeared as an heir.

### Why "Fred Meikle" = "Frederick Mägli/Magli" — PROBABLE-strong
Same first name and birth year (1848). Wife Mary Johnson/Johnsen, b. c.1858, married in Sturgeon Bay 1873/74. The same children appear (Carl, Oscar, Susan, Josie, Erka, Ernest, Iva) across 1900 "Mickel", 1905/1910 "Magli" and Emil's 1970 obituary. Same township. Emil's own 1902 marriage calls his parents Fred Meickle and Mary Johnsen. The illiteracy in 1880 matches the signature by mark in 1878. The surnames line up phonetically: Mägli ≈ "MAY-glee" became Magle/Maeghel/Mickel/Meikle.

### Swiss origin details
- **Country: CONFIRMED** (naturalization image) — Switzerland. Bavaria in 1880 and Germany in 1900 were the census takers' version of a German-speaking Swiss.
- **Arrival: CONFIRMED (image), year partly legible**: port of Milwaukee, October 1854 (or 1857). He would have been about 6, so he came with his family. FS tree: "Fredrick Marglez", 1860 census, Humboldt, Brown Co. (not checked).
- **Possible family in Door Co. — PROBABLE-weak:** **Conrad Magli**, 43, b. Switzerland, farmer, Nasewaupee, 1880 (FS MN4X-62N; wife Wilhelmine b. Germany, children Conrad, Godfred, Frederick, Wilhelmine, Julius, Bertha). His age fits a brother. Fred's father was also Conrad (1874 marriage).
- **Canton/parish — SPECULATIVE:** the FS tree (L2LT-3SN, "Frederick Magli 1848–1924") attaches a Swiss baptism, FS **68VG-TQVF**: **Samuel** Mägli, b. **19 Mar 1848**, bapt. 9 Apr 1848, **Oberbipp, Canton Bern**, parents **Konrad Mägli & Elisabeth Schorer**. The parents' given names match the 1874 marriage index (Conrad and Elizabeth) and the year matches. **But the child is "Samuel", not Friedrich**, and the same tree gives Konrad and Elisabeth an older son "Friedrich Mägli 1837–1916" (K484-7GK). To test it: (a) the Oberbipp register image (check for a second given name, or a Friedrich born 1848 to another Mägli couple); (b) a Mägli emigration from Oberbipp/Wangen district c.1854 (Bern emigration lists); (c) whether Conrad Magli of Nasewaupee was baptised at Oberbipp c.1836.
- **Death — SPECULATIVE:** the tree says 9 Aug 1924, Door Co. (no source). Next step: the Door archive, Aug 1924, under Magli/Meikle/Mickel.
