# Hutcheson/Hutchinson family: Thomas Hutcheson, Sophia Miller, and daughter Eliza (Walker / Peters / Martin)

Research date: 2026-10-08. Method: FamilySearch tree and record personas, read through a logged-in browser session (FamilySearch pages **require login**; WebFetch gets only a "browser not supported" shell), plus web searches. I looked at the 1850 census image myself. Other records are indexed only, or are tree-attached notes by contributor Carolynn Walker. Each finding is labelled with that confidence.

**Headline finding: Eliza's first husband was JOHN WALKER, not Martin Peters.** The married sequence is: (1) John Walker, by 1850 at Lockport NY, who died in Door Co c. 1859; (2) Martin Peters, m. 1859/60, who died in Door Co c. 1860–62; (3) Henry Martin, m. 12 Nov 1862. The children born 1853–1859 and listed as "Peters" in 1860 and "Martin" in 1870 are **Walker** children, so they are Eliza's own blood children. This reverses the local notes' hypothesis that they were "Martin Peters' children by an earlier wife." It also explains "Mrs. Chas. Walker" in Henry Martin's 1896 Card of Thanks: she was the wife of Eliza's son Charles Walker.

## Q1. The 1850 household: is there a Hutcheson household at Lockport with Thomas, Sophia and Eliza?

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED (image seen). In 1850 Eliza was **not** in her parents' household. She was already married, as "Eliza Walker," 21, b. Canada, wife of John Walker, 23, b. England, laborer. They were enumerated in the dwelling **immediately before** her parents, "Thomas Hutcherson," 38, b. England, laborer, and Sophia, 48, b. Canada, with a younger daughter **Rhoda, 5, b. Canada**. The place is Lockport, Niagara Co, NY, enumerated 27 July 1850.

### Inferences
- PROBABLE: Rhoda, born c. 1845 in Canada, shows the family was **still in Canada c. 1845** and moved to Lockport between 1845 and 1850. Eliza probably married John Walker c. 1848–50, either in Lockport or just before the move.
- Sophia's age varies: 48 in 1850 (b. c. 1802) against 1805 in the tree. Thomas's also varies: 38 (b. c. 1812) against 1808. Both are normal census drift.
- Rhoda, b. 1845, is ~15 years younger than Eliza. Children born between them are likely; none were in the 1850 household.

### Gaps
- No 1855 NY state census check was done. Lockport is in Niagara Co, and the 1855 schedules survive for most towns.
- I found no record of Thomas or Sophia after 1850. I don't know whether they followed Eliza to Wisconsin.

## Q2. Thomas Hutcheson's origin (England, or Scotland/Borders/Northumberland)

### Takeaway
Every record seen says **England**: the 1850 census, and Eliza's 1880 census entry, where her father's birthplace is England. The FS tree gives "24 Jul 1808, Middlesex, England," but the only basis for it is a "Legacy NFS Source" (old temple-submission data). That is UNSOURCED and should not be trusted. I found no evidence of Scotland or the Borders.

### Cited Findings
- Thomas Hutcherson, b. England, age 38 — 1850 census (image above). **CONFIRMED**.
- FS tree L8RF-177 gives birth as "24 Jul 1808, Middlesex, England." The supporting entry is "Legacy NFS Source: … Published information: birth: about 1808; Middlesex, England." There are only 3 sources: the 1850 census, the 1862 WI marriage, and that NFS entry — [FS tree L8RF-177](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/details/L8RF-177). **SPECULATIVE**. An NFS (New FamilySearch) legacy value is typically a patron submission with no record behind it.
- The 1862 marriage index renders the father's name as "Thomas Hutchison" — [XRLD-HZD](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRLD-HZD)

### Inferences
- PROBABLE: Thomas was English-born and a laborer. The "Hutcheson" spelling is not evidence of Scottish origin. The spellings used for this family are Hutcherson, Hutchenson, Hutchison, Hutchinson and Lizza Hutchenson (1901), and they vary by clerk.
- SPECULATIVE: the Middlesex claim may be a guess, possibly a mismatch with an English christening.

### Gaps
- The FS index searches I ran found no Upper Canada record of Thomas: no marriage to Sophia Miller (c. 1828–30), and no 1842 or 1851/52 Canada West census entry. Pre-1858 Upper Canada marriages are thinly indexed online.

## Q3. Where Thomas and Sophia lived in Canada; Eliza's actual birthplace

### Takeaway
The censuses consistently give **Canada** for Eliza (1850, 1870, and as recorded via the 1880 parents' fields). The 1860 census says England, which is an outlier. The FS tree's "**Niagara-on-the-Lake**" birthplace is **not supported by any of the 21 attached sources**, so treat it as SPECULATIVE. Lockport is a 1840s move point and fits a Niagara-peninsula origin, but no Canadian record was found.

### Cited Findings
- Eliza, 21, b. Canada — 1850 census (image above). **CONFIRMED**.
- The FS tree birth is "1830, Niagara-on-the-Lake, Niagara, Ontario." I reviewed the source list: the 1850, 1860, 1870 and 1880 censuses, the probate files, marriages of Henry Martin 1862, Thomas Melville 1878 and Charles Walker 1880, Michigan marriages 1895/96, the 1901 WI death of David W. Martin, the 1901 marriage of Eliphlet, FindAGrave, and "The History of Door County." None of these indexes a Niagara-on-the-Lake birthplace — [FS K814-2YL sources](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/sources/K814-2YL)
- In the FS tree, Sophia Miller MCXT-PV1 is given as "b. 1805, England." That contradicts the 1850 census (b. Canada) and Eliza's 1880 statement (mother b. Canada) — [FS tree MCXT-PV1](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/details/MCXT-PV1)
- A Niagara Falls Public Library index has a *different* Sophia Miller, née Riseley (1800–1838, first wife of Jacob Miller, buried Fort Erie). She is not this Sophia, but it shows Miller families on the Niagara frontier — [NFPL](https://nfpl.historicniagara.ca/s/All/item/375508)

### Inferences
- PROBABLE: Eliza was born c. 1829–30 in Upper Canada, most likely in the Niagara district given the later Lockport move. The specific town is unproven.
- SPECULATIVE: Sophia's parents William Miller and Margaret Laing are tagged Scottish/Northumbrian in the tree. If Sophia was Canadian-born c. 1802–05, that family was in Upper Canada by c. 1802.

### Gaps
- No Canadian record was located for any of the family. The 1842 Canada West census survives only partially, and the Niagara district coverage is spotty. The 1851/52 census is post-move, so they would not be in it.

## Q4. The Peters marriage, Martin Peters' death, and the Walker children

### Takeaway
Martin Peters was Eliza's **second** husband. John Walker died c. 1859 in Door County, and Eliza petitioned to administer his estate. She then married Martin Peters (the 1860 census marks both "married within the year"). Martin Peters died before late 1862, and Eliza administered his estate too (probate papers 1860–1862). The children's marriage records name their parents as **John Walker and Eliza (Martin)**.

### Inferences
- PROBABLE: John Walker (b. c. 1827 England) and Eliza had these children:
  - Elias, b. c. 1853 NY
  - Mary J, b. c. 1854 NY
  - Charles, b. c. 1856 WI
  - John W, b. c. 1858 WI
  - Eliza A, b. c. 1859 WI
- So the Walkers left Lockport for Door County c. 1854–56, and John Walker died c. 1859, probably in the Nasewaupee area. In 1860 the census enumerator gave all of them the stepfather's surname, Peters.
- The local-notes theory about "Martin Peters' children by an earlier wife" should be retired. The "married within the year" ticks fit a 1859/60 remarriage of the widow Walker.
- "William Portos/Peters, 18," in the 1880 Henry Martin household is plausibly the one child of Martin Peters and Eliza (b. c. 1861). This is SPECULATIVE until his records are found.
- Martin Peters probably died 1860–61. The probate items are dated 1860 and 1862 in the tree.

### Gaps
- I did not personally view the probate images, so their exact dates, John Walker's death date and Martin Peters' death date are unconfirmed.
- No Walker–Hutchinson marriage record was found (likely c. 1848–50, NY or Canada West).
- I found no record of a NY or WI marriage for Martin Peters and Eliza. Wisconsin had pre-1907 registration, so the WHS index should be checked for "Peters, Martin" in Door Co, 1859–60.
