# Records dismantle Don's assumed Scottish lines

None of Don Krumpos's Scottish ancestry is proven. The two links the local tree treats as Scottish both fail when checked against the records. **Margaret Laing of Rhynd** is attached to Sophia Miller only through a FamilySearch profile that merges two or three different couples. The real William Miller and Margaret Laing family stayed in Rhynd and Perth, baptising children there from 1804 to 1823. Sophia, by contrast, was **born in Canada c.1802**. **Fred Meikle** is almost certainly *not* a son of William Meikle of Stirlingshire: the 1914 obituary of William's eldest son lists every surviving brother, and Fred is missing even though he lived a few miles away. On the documented evidence, Don's lines through these families stop at Henry Martin (born near Belfast, 1830), Thomas Hutcheson (born in England, c.1812), Sophia Miller (born in Canada, c.1802), and Fred and Mary Meikle (origin unknown). The research did fill several real gaps. Eliza Hutchinson's **first husband was John Walker**, and Martin Peters was her second. The Walker children are her own. Henry Martin's sister was **Sarah Ann Martin**, who married John Kehoe in 1881 and died in 1915. Fred Meikle married about **March 1871**. Several unsupported claims in the tree should now be withdrawn. A Scottish strand is still possible through Sophia Miller's unknown Canadian parents, through Fred Meikle if he proves Scottish after all, or through an Ulster-Scots Martin line. All three are open questions, not findings. The cheapest records that would settle them are listed at the end.

## Sophia Miller was Canadian-born and does not belong to the Rhynd family

The tree's chain runs Margaret Laing (Rhynd, c.1783) → Sophia Miller → Eliza Hutchinson. That chain rests on a FamilySearch profile, **KH36-ZYD**, which gives Margaret's birth as "abt 1783, of Rhynd" with **zero sources**. The profile carries an alternate name "Margery" and a Collaborate note reading "1778" ([FS tree KH36-ZYD](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/details/KH36-ZYD)). The FamilySearch index has no Margaret Laing born in Rhynd between 1780 and 1786. The only Rhynd Margaret Laing indexed is one born **10 Oct 1776, daughter of Charles Laing and Grizzel Menzies** ([FS X13G-NVF](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:X13G-NVF)). That record is attached to a separate profile with no spouse ([FS 97F4-KFW](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/details/97F4-KFW)). "Of Rhynd" most likely comes from the children's Rhynd baptisms, not from any record of Margaret's own birth.

Those children show a family rooted in Scotland. **Margaret Millar was born 22 Apr 1807 in Rhynd** to William Millar and Margaret Laing ([FS X13G-J9F](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:X13G-J9F)). After that, Perth baptisms follow for Mathew in 1812, Robert in 1814, Ann in 1820 and William in 1823 ([FS FM91-HV6](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:FM91-HV6); [FS VQH5-1BN](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VQH5-1BN); [FS X19X-FXF](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:X19X-FXF); [FS X17V-LPF](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:X17V-LPF)). Two of the children died in Perth as adults. Jane Herdman died in 1872 aged 69, and Robert Miller, a grocer, died in 1874. Both death records name their parents as William Miller and Margaret Laing ([FS 6TG9-NXSV](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:6TG9-NXSV); [FS X95N-8X45](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:X95N-8X45)). Nothing indexed puts this couple in Canada.

Sophia's own records point the other way. In the **1850 census at Lockport, Niagara County, NY**, she is "Sophia Hutcherson, 48, Canada", which gives a birth around 1802 ([FS MCBJ-5WR](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MCBJ-5WR)). Her FamilySearch profile has just two sources, and neither names her parents ([FS MCXT-PV1 sources](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/sources/MCXT-PV1)). The only record giving her maiden name is her daughter's 1862 Door County marriage, which names "Thomas Hutchison and Sophia Miller" ([FS XRLD-HZ6](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRLD-HZ6)). A girl born in Canada c.1802 cannot comfortably belong to a couple who were baptising children in Rhynd in 1804 and 1807. Her birth would also fall in the same years as Jane/Jean (c.1803–04). KH36-ZYD lists 14 children, including a "Sophia 1805" with no source. It also absorbs a **Campsie, Stirlingshire 1802 baptism to William Millar and Margaret *Lang*** ([FS search](https://www.familysearch.org/search/record/results?q.fatherGivenName=William&q.fatherSurname=Miller&q.motherGivenName=Margaret&q.motherSurname=Laing&q.birthLikeDate.from=1798&q.birthLikeDate.to=1812)). That is the classic pattern of same-name profiles being merged. Sophia's link to this couple is **unsupported and very probably wrong**.

The **4 Sep 1799 marriage at St Nicholas, Newcastle upon Tyne**, makes things worse. The indexes give only "William Miller and **Margery** Laing", the date and the church. No ages, parishes, occupations or witnesses are indexed ([FS N6GN-L6F](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:N6GN-L6F); [FS QPQ1-D1J9](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QPQ1-D1J9)). The "2:3KT62RR" cited in the local notes is just the collection's internal source code. William's profile birth year of 1786 has no source. His Collaborate notes give conflicting years of 1774, 1779 and 1786 ([FS LZF4-CX5 collaborate](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/collaborate/LZF4-CX5)). A 13-year-old groom is impossible, so if this marriage is his, the 1786 date is wrong. Nothing ties the Newcastle couple to Perth or to Canada beyond two common names. It could be a third, unrelated couple. The local claim that the family "later emigrated to Canada" is a story built to join three records together. No record documents that emigration.

The upshot is that Sophia Miller's parents are **unknown**. The best guess (speculative) is a Niagara-district family in Upper Canada, whether Loyalist, late-Loyalist or British settler. That would fit her Canadian birth, a marriage c.1829 in what is now Ontario, and the later move just across the river to Lockport. The record most likely to name her father is the **Upper Canada marriage of Thomas Hutch\* and Sophia Miller, c.1825–1832**, which has not yet been found.

## Eliza Hutchinson married three times, and the "Peters" children were Walkers

The **1850 Lockport census image** fills the biggest gap on this line. Eliza, 21, born in Canada, was already married to **John Walker, 23, a labourer born in England**. They were enumerated in the dwelling immediately before her parents. The next household is Thomas Hutcherson, 38, a labourer born in England, with Sophia, 48, born in Canada, and a younger daughter **Rhoda, 5, born in Canada** ([FS image 3:1:S3HY-DYZQ-XCL](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:S3HY-DYZQ-XCL); [FS MCBJ-5WP](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MCBJ-5WP); [FS MCBJ-5WT](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MCBJ-5WT)). Rhoda's birth shows the family was still in Canada around 1845. Every record of Thomas says England. The "24 Jul 1808, Middlesex" on his profile is an unsourced legacy NFS entry ([FS L8RF-177](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/details/L8RF-177)). The Scottish reading of the "Hutcheson" spelling has no support either. The family's name appears as Hutcherson, Hutchenson, Hutchison and Hutchinson depending on the clerk. Eliza's FamilySearch birthplace of "Niagara-on-the-Lake" does not appear in any of her 21 attached sources ([FS K814-2YL sources](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/sources/K814-2YL)).

Two Door County marriage indexes reverse the local theory that the older children were Martin Peters's by an earlier wife. **Charles Walker**, born in Sturgeon Bay, married Elizabeth Schumacher on 31 Mar 1880. **Eliza Walker** married Thomas Melville on 7 Nov 1878. Both records name the parents as **"John Walker and Eliza Martin"** ([FS XRL8-G7Z](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRL8-G7Z); [FS XRL8-KQH](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRL8-KQH)). Eliza is given her surname at the time, Martin, not her maiden name. The Door County probate files attached to her profile include a petition by Eliza to administer the estate of her husband John Walker, and later papers for the estate of her "late husband Martin Peters". These have been read from contributor notes only; the images have not been viewed ([FS 3QSQ-G9G7-P34V](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QSQ-G9G7-P34V); [FS 3QSQ-G9G7-P93L](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QSQ-G9G7-P93L)). The sequence is therefore:

- **John Walker**, married by 1850, died in Door County c.1859.
- **Martin Peters**, a farmer born in Ireland, married 1859/60 (the 1860 census marks them "married within the year"), died by 1862.
- **Henry Martin**, married 12 Nov 1862 at Sturgeon Bay ([FS XRLD-HZ8](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRLD-HZ8)).

The children listed as "Peters" in 1860 and "Martin" in 1870 are Elias (c.1853, NY), Mary J (c.1854, NY), Charles (c.1856), John W (c.1858) and Eliza A (c.1859). They were **Eliza's own Walker children** and Henry's stepchildren. "William Portos/Peters, 18" in the 1880 household is plausibly the one Peters child (speculative). Eliza's maiden name appears again in her later children's records: "Eliza Hutchison" in Henry Jr.'s 1895/96 Michigan marriage, "Lizza Hutchenson" in David W. Martin's 1901 death record, and "Elizabeth Hutchinson" in Eliphlet's 1901 marriage ([FS FCT1-QQR](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:FCT1-QQR); [FS XLC8-2HH](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XLC8-2HH); [FS XRVC-8L6](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRVC-8L6)). Eliza died on 17 Oct 1884 in Sevastopol and was buried at Bayside ([FS K814-2YL](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/details/K814-2YL)).

## Fred Meikle is missing from the Scottish Meikles' own sibling list

The local tree calls the Meikle line "RESOLVED SCOTTISH" on the strength of a shared surname, a shared town and a shared cemetery. The deciding document is the **Door County Democrat of 10 Apr 1914, "Death of Alex Meikle"** (doc `0a8b94b5-5745-457c-a604-522158934fc2/wsbd0000/20120718/00001109`, read from the page image). Alexander was William's eldest son, born **18 Nov 1839 in Stirlingshire**. The obituary says he was survived by "four brothers, James and John of Sturgeon Bay town, Dan of Spokane Bridge, Washington, and Thomas … and one sister, Mrs. McBride, of Milton, N. D." ([Door County archive](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)). Fred was alive and local at the time. "Mr. and Mrs. Fred Meikle" appear in the Sawyer column in March 1917 (doc `…/20120718/00001261`). An obituary that names a brother in Washington state and a sister in North Dakota would hardly leave out a living brother across town. William's own 1893 obituary appeared in the same issue as an item about "Fred Meikle" building a saloon at Tornado. It calls William only "father of Alex Meikle" (doc `7f27568d-68b1-4968-bf42-eda452e4b5f4/wsbd0000/20120522/00000883`). William's FindAGrave page links only Alexander, James and John ([FindAGrave #130602798](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/130602798)). The Fred → William link should be treated as **disproven with high, though not absolute, confidence**. A nephew relationship remains possible but has no evidence behind it.

The same archive adds new facts about Fred's own family:

- **Marriage date.** A 10 Mar 1911 item reports a celebration of "the fortieth anniversary of their wedding" at the home of Mr. and Mrs. Fred Meikel. That puts the marriage at about **March 1871** (doc `…/20120718/00000948`). It also explains why searches of the 1875–81 Wisconsin marriage index found nothing.
- **More children.** The 3 Mar 1911 column names **Ella, Bertha and Julius Meikel** and a married daughter, **Mrs. Kloudy**, in the Town of Sturgeon Bay (doc `…/00000947`).
- **Origin.** These are German-pattern names. Combined with the spelling drift (Meikel, Mickles, Melkel, Mielke), they make a German Mielke or Michel origin, later anglicized, a live hypothesis. It remains speculative until a census shows Fred's birthplace.
- **Another Meikle family.** A separate Meikle/Mikels family also lived in the town (1956 notice, doc `1e8fc801-90a4-4104-8e86-19a1ea0947dc/wsbd0000/20141103/00001127`) and must be kept apart from both of the others.

**Mary Johnsen's** birthplace and parents remain unknown. Her maiden name still rests only on Emil's FindAGrave memorial, as recorded in the local notes. Every "Mary Meikle" hit in the archive turned out to be a tax notice, a different woman, or Emil's obituary. The William Meikle line itself is now better placed. Its origin is **Stirlingshire**, not Glasgow city. The family emigrated in 1848, lived in New York until 1856, then Toronto, then Chesley, Ontario. That line is worth pursuing only if Fred is somehow tied back into it.

## Henry Martin's Ulster origin is real, but his "Scots-Irish" label is not proven

The only birthplace evidence is still Henry's 1896 Advocate obituary: born "at or near the city of Belfast, Ireland, August 18, 1830". He emigrated in 1851 and reached Sturgeon Bay in July 1857 ([Door County archive](https://archive.co.door.wi.us), Advocate 1896-08-15 p1). No Irish baptism or Martin–Storm marriage was found. However, the FamilySearch control searches returned zero results even for very broad queries, so those negatives are **unreliable** ([FS control search](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/record/results?q.surname=Martin&q.fatherGivenName=Henry&q.anyPlace=Ireland&q.anyDate.from=1820&q.anyDate.to=1845&count=100)).

The parents' names "Henry Martin and Nancy Storm" appear in the index of the 1862 marriage ([FS XRLD-HZ6](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRLD-HZ6)), but nobody has viewed the register image itself. There is also a discrepancy over who performed the ceremony. The contemporaneous notice, reprinted in 1912, names Robert Graham, Esq. The 1896 obituary names Judge M. E. Lyman (Advocate 1912-11-14 p2). Storm is close to absent from Ulster records. It looks like a rare name of English origin ([britishsurnames.uk](https://britishsurnames.uk/surname/storm)), not a Scottish planter name. That leaves two readings. A genuine Storm would point to an English incomer family in Belfast. A misreading of **Stormont/Stormonth**, a Perthshire locational name found around Belfast, would point back toward Scotland. The religious evidence leans slightly toward Church of Ireland or Methodist rather than Presbyterian. Henry's funeral was taken by **Rev. David Lewis, the Methodist Episcopal pastor**, and both family marriages were civil. Irish Methodism of the 1830s drew mainly on Anglican, not Presbyterian, stock. Confidence in that lean is low, and the newspaper archive gives no specific URL for the Lewis items beyond the archive itself.

The new lead is Henry's sister. The Advocate of 3 Nov 1881 reports "KEHOE—MARTIN.—At Bailey's Harbor, Wis., Oct. 24, 1881 … Mr. John Kehoe and **Miss Sarah Ann Martin**", and describes the bride as "a sister of Henry Martin, Esq., the popular Sevastopol postmaster" (Advocate 1881-11-03 p3; [WHS MR2619003](https://www.wisconsinhistory.org/record/vital/MR2619003/kehoe-john)). She died at North Bay on 8 Mar 1915, "a lady of 60 years" (Door County News 1915-03-10 p1, [archive](https://archive.co.door.wi.us)). That age makes her about 24 years younger than Henry. She may have been a half-sister, or the parents may have emigrated after him. Either way, her **1915 Wisconsin death certificate**, from the period after statewide registration began, should name both parents and their birthplaces. It is the cheapest independent test of "Nancy Storm".

### The Mrs. Melville conflict: the newspaper's "aunt" is loose usage

The Martin notes quote the Advocate of 29 Nov 1902: "Mrs. Kehoe is an aunt of Mr. Martin and Mrs. Melville". They conclude that Mrs. Melville was Henry's own daughter. The Hutcheson evidence outweighs this. Mrs. Thomas Melville was **Eliza Walker**. Her 1878 marriage index names her parents as **John Walker and Eliza Martin** ([FS XRL8-KQH](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XRL8-KQH)). She was "Eliza A Peters, 1" in the 1860 census, born about 1859, three years before Henry married her mother. That makes her **Henry's stepdaughter** and the half-sister of "Mr. Martin" (Henry Lincoln Martin). Mrs. Kehoe was therefore her step-aunt. A village correspondent writing a social note about a visiting couple would naturally call Mrs. Kehoe the "aunt" of two siblings raised in the same house. A marriage register entry, filled in at the time from the couple's own statement of parentage, is much stronger evidence of biological parentage than a newspaper's kinship label 24 years later. The "biological child of Henry Sr." conclusion should be **dropped**. Nothing about Henry's Irish origin depends on it.

## Conclusion

Both "Scottish" links were inferred from surnames, Laing and Meikle, and both broke apart once the surrounding records were read. The records themselves show something different: an English labourer and a Canadian-born wife on the Niagara frontier, a three-times-married daughter whose Walker children were raised as Martins, and an Ulster immigrant whose much younger sister followed him to Door County. Don's Scottish ancestry is now an open question rather than a documented line. It can only be reopened through new records, not by re-reading the old ones.

The practical lesson for the tree is that FamilySearch profiles with zero-source birth fields and many merged children are where errors come in. Items 1 through 3 above cost little and could each settle a whole branch within weeks. If item 6 finds Sophia's Upper Canada marriage, that is the last plausible route to a proven Scottish (or Loyalist) ancestor on the Hutchinson side.
